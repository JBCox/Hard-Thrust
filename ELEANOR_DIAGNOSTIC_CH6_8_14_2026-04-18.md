### Investigator-cluster watch — borderline (no fix required)

Two phrases read more like a detective screening a suspect than a predator selecting prey. Together they're *close* to the 3+ cluster threshold but don't cross it:

- **L1651** — "The gaps in his stories he fills with vague explanations." A hunter tracks weakness, not story inconsistencies. This phrase is the voice of someone verifying a cover, not tasting a target.
- **L1677** — "But I've learned that people can sound real while lying about everything." "Lying" is the investigator's vocabulary (truth vs falsehood). A predator framing would be about masks, rot under pretty surfaces, innocence as camouflage.
- **L1675** — "I ask about his past, probing for weaknesses." Borderline — "weaknesses" pulls it back to predator. Fine as-is.

These survive because the surrounding predator language is strong and the chapter context (she's a seductive groundskeeper with a secret) gives the reader a non-investigator frame to land on. But a first-time reader who is *already suspicious* of Eleanor's real role could seize on L1651 + L1677 as tells.

**Optional LOW fix** if you ever want to tighten this:
- L1677 `"people can sound real while lying about everything"` → `"people can taste innocent while rot runs beneath the skin"` (keeps the ambiguity but shifts the metaphor from truth-detection to predator-taste).
- Leave L1651 as-is — "gaps in his stories" is generic enough that in a thriller voice it reads as paranoia not tradecraft.

### Note on drugging misdirection (this chapter is load-bearing)

Ch 14 is the chapter where Eleanor explicitly confirms she's drugging Josh ("Whatever I gave him, it hit harder than I expected — came back disoriented, scratched up, bruised" — L4632). That confession is *critical misdirection*: it gives the reader a concrete non-DID explanation for Josh's blackouts before the real explanation lands in Ch 25. Do not weaken this. It's working exactly right.

Small internal logic check: drugs alone don't cause scratches and bruises, but "disoriented person walking in woods in the dark for 7 hours" absolutely does, and Eleanor's line reads as her attributing the physical damage to *her own drugs hitting harder than planned* — which is the correct frame for a reader in Ch 14 to hold. Clean.

Not a spoiler concern. Flagging as a copy-edit pass item only if a future round is focused on prose polish — regularizing those two lines to present tense would smooth a small rhythmic hitch. Example fix:
